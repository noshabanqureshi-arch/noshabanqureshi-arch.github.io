# Square Social Studio — Brand Operating System

## Brand purpose
Square Social Studio helps busy service-based small-business owners turn what they already know into clear, professional content without making content creation their full-time job.

## Positioning statement
For busy service-based small-business owners who know their work but struggle to consistently communicate it online, Square Social Studio is the practical content-systems brand that turns expertise into a repeatable month of useful content, so they can show up professionally without living on social media, because every product combines simple strategy, editable tools and human-guided AI workflows.

## Brand promise
**Show Up Simply™**

Turn what you already know about your business into useful, professional content through a calm, repeatable system.

## Core method
**Clarify → Plan → Create → Publish → Repeat**

Every product, course, article, email and sales page should map to at least one stage of this method.

## Primary audience
Busy women running service-based or expertise-led small businesses. They are capable at their actual work but inconsistent or overwhelmed with content planning, writing, Reels, design and publishing.

### Primary jobs-to-be-done
- Know what to post.
- Turn expertise into content without sounding generic.
- Create content faster.
- Look professional and consistent.
- Use AI without losing authenticity or inventing expertise.
- Connect content to real business outcomes.

### Main pain points
- Blank-page paralysis.
- Too many disconnected content ideas.
- Inconsistent visual identity.
- Fear of sounding salesy or artificial.
- Limited time for content creation.
- Uncertainty about what metrics matter.

## Brand voice
### Personality
- Calm
- Capable
- Human
- Clear
- Practical
- Thoughtful
- Encouraging without hype

### Tone
The voice stays stable; tone adjusts to context.
- Teaching: clear, patient, concise.
- Sales: confident, specific, low-pressure.
- Support: warm, direct, solution-focused.
- Social: conversational and useful.
- Crisis/correction: factual, accountable, calm.

### Language rules
Use plain English. Prefer short, concrete sentences. Explain jargon when unavoidable. Speak to one person using “you.” Use active voice. Make benefits specific. Use Canadian spelling where natural. Prioritize clarity over cleverness.

### Avoid
- Fake urgency or scarcity.
- Overpromising income, reach or results.
- Generic motivational filler.
- “Crush it,” “dominate,” “hustle,” “game-changing,” “secret hack,” and similar hype language.
- Invented testimonials, case studies, statistics or credentials.
- Robotic AI phrases and unnecessary em dashes.
- Talking down to beginners.

### Signature phrases
- Show Up Simply™
- Clear over clever.
- Useful over noisy.
- A system you can repeat.
- Turn what you already know into content.
- You do not need to become a full-time creator.

## Copy structure
For social captions and short promotional copy, use:
**Support → Provide → Direct**
1. Support: hook that complements the visual rather than repeating it.
2. Provide: useful educational, emotional or logistical value.
3. Direct: one clear CTA.

For teaching captions, use:
**Hook → Value → Example → Action**

For Reels, use:
**Hook → 3 Points → Next Step**

## Visual identity
### Master colours
- Navy: `#0B1F3A` — primary authority, trust and structure.
- Navy Soft: `#15304F` — secondary dark surface.
- Cream: `#F8F6F0` — main background; calm and approachable.
- Warm White: `#FFFDF8` — cards and readable surfaces.
- Gold: `#D9B66D` — primary accent; warmth, value and attention.
- Sage: `#8FAE9E` — supportive accent; calm, progress and practicality.
- Coral: `#D58B79` — occasional emphasis, human warmth and video/reel products.
- Sky: `#A8C8D1` — occasional informational accent.
- Ink: `#142036` — main text.
- Muted: `#657083` — secondary text.
- Line: `#DCD8CE` — borders and dividers.

### Colour theory/application
Navy is the dominant anchor and should carry authority and trust. Cream/white provide generous negative space and reduce visual overwhelm. Gold is the principal action/accent colour and should be used sparingly so it retains attention value. Sage supports calm, growth and systems. Coral and sky are product-category accents, not competing primary brand colours.

Recommended ratio for most layouts: approximately 60% cream/white, 25% navy/ink, 10% gold/sage, 5% coral/sky or other accents.

Never use low-contrast gold body text on cream or white. Use navy/ink for readable text. Test important text/buttons for accessible contrast.

## Typography
### Display / editorial headings
`Georgia, "Times New Roman", serif`

Use for H1/H2, product-cover names, major pull quotes and high-level campaign titles.

### Body / UI
`Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif`

Use for paragraphs, buttons, labels, navigation, forms, captions, worksheets and course support text.

### Typography rules
- Sentence case for headings except small eyebrow labels.
- Eyebrows: uppercase, small size, wide tracking.
- Avoid more than two font families in a single asset.
- Maintain generous line-height for body copy.
- Never shrink instructional text merely to fit a layout; simplify the copy instead.

## Imagery and graphic language
Use calm editorial compositions, real small-business workspaces, hands/process imagery, screen demonstrations and simple geometric square motifs. Prefer useful visual examples over generic stock imagery. Keep backgrounds uncluttered. Products may use controlled accent colours while retaining navy typography or structure.

Avoid overly glossy corporate imagery, fake luxury imagery, unrelated lifestyle stock, visual clutter and stereotypical “girlboss” aesthetics.

## Logo usage
Use the Square Social Studio square-grid icon plus wordmark where possible. Maintain clear space around the mark. Do not recolour the primary mark outside approved navy/cream/white variations. Do not stretch, rotate or apply shadows/effects to the logo.

## Funnel architecture
1. Awareness — free educational content and lead magnet.
2. Consideration — $27 starter product / useful email nurture.
3. Decision — $69 mini-course / $149 toolkit.
4. Transformation — $349 flagship system.
5. Engagement & advocacy — follow-up education, customer wins, referral/testimonial requests and future recurring products.

## Paid / owned / earned media
- Owned: website, email list, blog/resources, downloadable products, courses and brand social accounts.
- Paid: use only after an offer converts organically; advertise lead magnet or proven offer rather than boosting random content.
- Earned: customer shares, reviews, backlinks, referrals and user-generated examples with permission.

## Content pillars
1. **Teach** — practical education, FAQs, tutorials, myths and mistakes.
2. **Trust** — process, proof, methods, behind the scenes and transparent decisions.
3. **Connect** — founder philosophy, relatable business realities, values and stories.
4. **Offer** — what the product/service is, who it is for, benefits, inclusions and objections.
5. **Convert** — one clear next step: download, buy, join, book or inquire.

## Platform roles
- Instagram: visual brand building, Reels, carousels, Stories and product education.
- TikTok/Shorts: concise educational video and discovery.
- YouTube: deeper educational content and searchable tutorials when capacity allows.
- Pinterest: evergreen discovery for templates, planning and content-system topics.
- LinkedIn: selective thought leadership if targeting consultants, coaches and B2B service businesses.
- Email: primary owned conversion and retention channel.

Do not create or maintain a platform unless the business can support it consistently.

## Video standards
- Clear over clever titles.
- Native uploads on the destination platform.
- Strong, readable thumbnails.
- Build one master idea, then waterfall it into shorter assets.
- Use captions/subtitles.
- AI voice/avatar is production support, not the value proposition.
- Use screen recordings and real examples for teaching whenever possible.

## Customer experience principles
- Reduce choices and cognitive load.
- Always explain what happens next.
- Make files easy to find and use.
- Keep paid content protected outside the public GitHub repository.
- Use transparent prices and inclusions.
- Do not hide material conditions in small print.

## SMART business goals and KPIs
### Validation phase
Goal: validate the first funnel before expanding the catalogue.
- Lead-magnet conversion rate.
- Email welcome-sequence engagement.
- Starter-product conversion rate.
- Mini-course conversion rate.
- Average order value.
- Refund rate.
- Customer support questions by category.

### Growth phase
- Qualified website sessions.
- Email subscriber growth.
- Revenue by offer.
- Revenue per visitor/subscriber.
- Funnel progression from free → paid → upgrade.
- Repeat purchase rate.
- Customer acquisition cost if paid media begins.

Prioritize business outcomes over vanity metrics. Likes and follower count are diagnostic signals, not the final goal.

## Editorial quality checklist
Before publishing, verify:
1. Does this serve a defined audience need?
2. Which funnel stage does it support?
3. Which content pillar does it belong to?
4. Is the hook clear rather than clever?
5. Does the visual follow approved colours and typography?
6. Does the copy sound calm, capable, human and practical?
7. Is there one clear CTA?
8. Are facts, examples and claims true?
9. Is the layout readable on mobile?
10. Is the asset accessible: contrast, captions/alt text where applicable, readable font size?
11. What KPI will tell us whether it worked?
