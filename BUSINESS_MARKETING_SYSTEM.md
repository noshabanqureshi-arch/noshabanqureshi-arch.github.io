# Square Social Studio — Business & Marketing Operating System

This file turns the strongest strategies from the Conestoga Social Media Marketing courses into operating rules for Square Social Studio.

## 1. Business design constraint
Square Social Studio is not a high-volume agency.

Operating guardrails:
- Owner workload target: maximum 20–25 hours per week.
- Maximum active done-for-you clients: 3.
- Revenue growth should come primarily from pricing, digital products, courses, recurring membership and reuse of intellectual property—not from continuously adding client work.
- Default to asynchronous delivery, approvals and support.
- Every new offer must be evaluated for owner hours required per sale and per month.
- Premium client delivery must follow `CLIENT_DELIVERY_CAPACITY_SYSTEM.md`; the calendar drives delivery rather than the inbox.
- Target premium-client owner time is approximately 12–15 hours/client/month, with the remaining workweek protected for scalable offers, Square Social marketing, admin and buffer.

## 2. Business → marketing → website goal hierarchy

### Business goal
Build a profitable, low-overhead education and content-systems business for small-business owners while keeping owner workload inside the operating guardrails.

### Marketing goals
1. Build a qualified owned audience of small-business owners.
2. Convert useful free education into low-risk first purchases.
3. Progress satisfied buyers into higher-value education, recurring support or premium done-for-you service when appropriate.
4. Increase repeat purchases, referrals, reviews and advocacy so growth does not depend only on acquiring new people.

### Website goals
The website has five jobs:
1. Explain the Square Social positioning quickly.
2. Capture qualified demand through free resources.
3. Sell digital products and courses.
4. Grow the planned Vault membership.
5. Qualify a maximum of three premium client partnerships.

### SMART measurement rule
Every campaign must connect:
**Business goal → marketing goal → website objective → KPI → action.**

Do not launch campaigns with a goal such as “get more engagement.” Define what engagement is expected to lead to and how it will be measured.

## 3. Segmentation, targeting and positioning

### Segment A — DIY Starter
**Situation:** Owner/operator is doing the marketing herself and wants a fast, affordable solution.

**Primary jobs:**
- Know what to post.
- Stop starting from a blank page.
- Get a professional-looking plan quickly.

**Best offers:**
- Free resources
- $27 30-Day Content Starter
- focused downloads

**Message:** You already know your business. Use a simple system to turn that knowledge into content.

### Segment B — Guided Builder
**Situation:** Owner is willing to do the work but wants a clear process, teaching and reusable tools.

**Primary jobs:**
- Learn a repeatable content system.
- Use AI without sounding generic.
- Batch and repurpose content.
- Build a consistent brand and workflow.

**Best offers:**
- $69 mini-course
- $149 Show Up Simply™ Toolkit
- $349 Show Up Simply™ Content System
- planned Square Social Vault

**Message:** Learn the system once, then repeat it without rebuilding your strategy every week.

### Segment C — Premium Outsourcer
**Situation:** Established service/expertise-led business values time more than doing content production internally.

**Primary jobs:**
- Remove the monthly content scramble.
- Keep strategy connected to real business goals.
- Reduce meetings and approval burden.

**Best offer:**
- Done-for-You Content System, maximum 3 active partnerships

**Message:** Give us the business knowledge and priorities. We turn them into a structured monthly content system.

### Targeting rule
Prioritize service-based and expertise-led businesses where:
- the owner has genuine expertise to communicate,
- customer questions naturally generate educational content,
- a customer is worth enough that better marketing has meaningful business value,
- the owner can be reached through digital channels,
- the offer does not depend on constant trend-chasing.

### Positioning
Square Social Studio competes primarily on **clarity, convenience and a repeatable system**, not on being the cheapest agency or promising viral growth.

Core positioning:
> Turn what you already know into useful, professional content—without making social media your full-time job.

## 4. The 7P service mix

### Product
A connected ladder of:
- free education,
- digital downloads,
- self-paced courses,
- toolkit/flagship education,
- recurring Vault support,
- limited done-for-you content systems.

### Price
Use a value ladder rather than one giant purchase:
- free → low-ticket → guided → flagship → recurring → premium.
- Prices must be transparent.
- Do not rely on fake discounts or manufactured urgency.

### Place
Primary owned destination: Square Social Studio website.

Commerce/delivery: approved checkout and course/digital-delivery platforms.

Discovery channels are selected based on the target segment rather than trying to maintain every platform.

### Promotion
Priority order:
1. useful owned content,
2. search/evergreen discovery,
3. email nurture,
4. earned reviews/referrals/UGC,
5. paid promotion only after an offer or message shows organic evidence of converting.

### People
The brand should feel calm, capable and human regardless of whether delivery is founder-led, AI-assisted or supported by future contractors.

Anyone representing the brand must follow the same voice, privacy, accessibility, claims and customer-service standards.

### Process
Design for low friction:
- one obvious next step,
- transparent inclusions,
- automatic digital delivery where possible,
- asynchronous onboarding and approvals,
- clear support boundaries,
- documented workflows,
- no unnecessary meetings,
- predictable monthly production windows for premium clients,
- real-data reporting rather than estimated performance claims.

### Physical evidence
For a digital business, proof includes:
- a professional, consistent website,
- useful free resources,
- polished previews,
- clear policies,
- reliable delivery,
- real customer reviews or examples only when permission exists,
- visible quality and consistency across channels.

## 5. Customer journey
Square Social uses two compatible models.

### Customer-facing story
**Discover → Learn → Trust → Act**

### Full business funnel
1. **Awareness / Discover** — useful social, search, video, referrals.
2. **Consideration / Learn** — free resources, tutorials, blog/resource content, email.
3. **Decision / Trust + Act** — product pages, course pages, comparisons, transparent proof and FAQs.
4. **Transformation** — customer completes/uses the product or service and gets the promised outcome.
5. **Engagement & retention** — helpful follow-up, implementation support, Vault, complementary products.
6. **Advocacy** — voluntary reviews, referrals, shares and user-generated examples with permission.

The funnel does not stop at purchase.

## 6. Integrated marketing rule
One campaign idea should appear consistently across the channels that make sense for the target customer.

Example:
**Theme:** Turn customer questions into 30 days of content.

- Search/resource article: explain the method.
- YouTube/tutorial: demonstrate it.
- Reels/Shorts: 3–5 excerpts.
- Carousel: visual framework.
- Pinterest: evergreen graphics.
- Email: teach one part and link to the resource/offer.
- Free guide: capture qualified interest.
- $27 Starter: next paid step.

Do not create unrelated campaigns for every channel. Reuse the strategy and adapt the format.

## 7. Content inventory before new creation
Before creating new content, check:
1. Existing website pages
2. Existing course lessons
3. Existing downloads/workbooks
4. Old social posts
5. Customer questions/support themes
6. Email content
7. Existing video/audio
8. Earned reviews/mentions that can legally and ethically be reused

Classify assets:
- **Owned** — website, email, courses, downloads, brand accounts.
- **Earned** — reviews, backlinks, shares, referrals, customer-created examples.
- **Paid** — ads, sponsored content, paid creators.

For each asset record:
- topic,
- audience segment,
- funnel stage,
- format,
- channel,
- CTA,
- performance,
- keep/update/repurpose/retire decision.

## 8. Channel roles

### Website / resources
Owned authority, SEO discovery, conversion and product education.

### Email
Primary owned nurture, conversion and retention channel. Build the list with explicit, documented permission processes appropriate to the communication being sent.

Mailchimp is the selected platform, but ongoing marketing automation remains off until the sender/business-address, consent, unsubscribe, segmentation and privacy gates in `EMAIL_PLATFORM_ACTIVATION.md` are complete.

### Instagram
Visual teaching, Reels, carousels, product education and brand familiarity.

### TikTok / Shorts
Short educational discovery when capacity supports it.

### YouTube
Searchable tutorials and deeper teaching when a topic benefits from demonstration.

### Pinterest
Evergreen discovery for templates, planning, checklists and content-system topics.

### LinkedIn
Selective B2B thought leadership when targeting consultants and professional services.

Do not maintain a channel simply because it exists.

## 9. Paid media rule
Do not pay to amplify an unproven message.

Before paid promotion:
- offer is complete and delivers correctly,
- landing page is mobile-ready,
- conversion action can be measured,
- organic traffic/content has produced useful evidence,
- customer support and refund/delivery processes work,
- budget and stop conditions are defined.

Best early paid target: a proven lead magnet or proven entry product, not random boosted posts.

For the live $27 Starter, campaign traffic should use the focused landing page documented in `FIRST_1000_CAMPAIGN_ROUTING.md` rather than defaulting every visitor to the normal product page.

## 10. Customer feedback and advocacy
After a customer has had enough time to use an offer:
- ask whether it solved the intended problem,
- collect one concrete improvement suggestion,
- periodically use a simple recommendation/NPS-style question,
- invite an honest review without requiring positivity,
- ask explicit permission before using a customer quote, image, result or content in marketing.

Never invent testimonials or manufacture social proof.

## 11. Privacy and accessibility operating rules
- Collect only information needed for the stated purpose.
- Keep consent and unsubscribe processes documented for commercial email.
- Keep paid/customer data out of the public website repository.
- Update privacy disclosures when new analytics, email, advertising or customer-data tools are added.
- Design toward WCAG 2.2 AA principles where practical: perceivable content, keyboard operability, clear navigation, accessible forms, sufficient contrast, captions/transcripts and usable mobile targets.

## 12. Decision filter for every new idea
Before adding a new platform, product, service or campaign ask:
1. Which audience segment is this for?
2. Which customer problem does it solve?
3. Which funnel stage does it support?
4. What is the one CTA?
5. What existing asset can be reused?
6. What KPI will show whether it worked?
7. What ongoing owner hours does it create?
8. Can it be automated, templated or delegated later?

If those answers are unclear, do not add it yet.
