# Square Social Studio — Client Delivery & Capacity System

Purpose: protect the founder workload ceiling while delivering a premium, predictable done-for-you service for a maximum of three active clients.

This system combines the strongest parts of the earlier Square Social operating system with the current business model: fewer premium clients, more digital intellectual property, asynchronous delivery, batch production and evidence-based reporting.

## 1. Non-negotiable capacity rules

- Maximum owner workload target: **20–25 hours/week**.
- Planning baseline: **about 87 owner-hours/month** (4 hours × 5 weekdays × 4.33 weeks).
- Maximum active done-for-you clients: **3**.
- Target owner delivery time per premium client: **12–15 hours/month**.
- Target total premium-client delivery time at three clients: **36–45 hours/month**.
- Do not use the remaining hours to add client #4. Protect them for products, courses, Vault production, Square Social marketing, admin and buffer.
- If a client exceeds the agreed time budget for two consecutive months, re-scope, raise price, delegate production or end the mismatch.
- No unlimited revisions, daily on-call community management or open-ended “can you quickly…” work.

## 2. Monthly owner-hour budget

Conservative planning example at three clients:

| Work category | Monthly owner-hour target |
| --- | ---: |
| Premium client delivery | 36–45 |
| Square Social marketing / audience growth | 10–12 |
| Digital products / courses / Vault | 12–18 |
| Admin / finance / systems | 6–8 |
| Buffer / catch-up / unexpected work | 8–12 |
| **Total** | **72–95** |

The business should normally operate below the theoretical 100% time ceiling. Buffer is capacity, not wasted time.

## 3. Capacity alarms

Review monthly. Flag the business when any of these are true:

- owner workload exceeds 80% of the planned ceiling for two consecutive months;
- premium delivery exceeds 45 owner-hours/month;
- one client repeatedly exceeds 15 owner-hours/month without corresponding price/scope adjustment;
- revisions exceed the contracted limit repeatedly;
- urgent/reactive work replaces scheduled batch work;
- digital-product/course/Vault development receives less than 10 owner-hours/month for two consecutive months;
- Square Social owned marketing stops because client work consumed the week;
- effective owner revenue per delivery hour falls while scope is unchanged.

Response order:
1. remove unnecessary work;
2. template/automate;
3. tighten scope;
4. raise price;
5. delegate repeatable production;
6. only then consider any new service capacity.

## 4. Calendar-driven monthly delivery cycle

The calendar drives the work—not the inbox.

### Days 1–3 — EVALUATE
- collect real platform/site/business analytics supplied or exported for the client;
- review outcomes first: qualified inquiries, calls, bookings, purchases, direction requests, DMs or other agreed commercial signals;
- review diagnostic metrics second: reach, saves, watch time, clicks, profile actions and engagement where relevant;
- produce KEEP / STOP / TEST / SCALE decisions;
- if required data is missing, request it rather than estimating results.

### Days 4–7 — PLAN
- confirm one or two business priorities for the next month;
- update customer questions, offers, objections and seasonal context;
- choose the month’s Discover → Learn → Trust → Act balance;
- lock content themes, campaign focus and no more than two meaningful tests.

### Days 8–14 — PRODUCE
- extract ideas from the client’s actual materials first;
- batch copy, graphics, Reel scripts and reusable campaign assets;
- use templates and AI for organization/drafting, with human fact/voice/QC review;
- reuse strong existing assets before creating from zero;
- prepare the full approval batch together wherever practical.

### Days 15–18 — APPROVE + SCHEDULE
- send one organized approval batch rather than drip-feeding content;
- use the contracted revision window;
- schedule only approved content;
- unresolved factual/compliance questions return to the client before publication.

### Days 19–30 — PUBLISH + LIGHT SUPPORT
- content goes live according to the calendar;
- complete only the support/community tasks explicitly included in scope;
- collect customer questions and performance observations for next month;
- protect the next production cycle from ad-hoc additions.

## 5. Two-week buffer rule

Once a client is stable, aim to keep approximately two weeks of approved or nearly approved content ahead.

The purpose is resilience:
- illness or family disruption should not immediately become a missed client deadline;
- one difficult week should be absorbed by the system;
- client urgency should not control the founder’s day.

Do not achieve the buffer by working nights/weekends as a normal operating model.

## 6. 14-day onboarding system

A new premium client should move contract-to-ready in a structured 14-day window.

### Days 1–2 — setup
- signed agreement and first payment confirmed;
- intake completed;
- client folder/workspace created;
- access requirements listed;
- no shared passwords; use delegated/platform access and 2FA where available.

### Days 3–5 — business + brand intake
- review website, offers, existing social, customer questions and existing assets;
- record brand voice, factual boundaries, claims/compliance issues and approval contacts;
- identify gaps that must be collected before content begins.

### Days 6–9 — strategy
- confirm 90-day objective;
- define primary audience/customer problem;
- choose positioning, content pillars and channel roles;
- agree what Square Social will not do.

### Days 10–12 — first batch
- prepare first content batch;
- complete internal quality control;
- send for client review in one organized approval window.

### Days 13–14 — go-live preparation
- apply agreed revisions;
- schedule approved content;
- confirm reporting inputs;
- confirm next monthly cycle dates.

No go-live before the required business facts, approvals and initial content plan are complete.

## 7. Client data isolation

Each client must have a separate private client workspace outside the public website repository.

Never:
- place client credentials, private analytics, customer lists, private brand files or paid client assets in the public GitHub repository;
- reuse private client data across clients;
- paste confidential client information into public examples;
- use one client’s results as another client’s proof without explicit permission and proper anonymization/attribution.

## 8. Reporting standard

Reports use **real data only**.

If data is absent, the output is a request list—not invented estimates.

Client reporting order:
1. business outcomes where available;
2. agreed conversion/intent signals;
3. content/channel diagnostics;
4. what worked;
5. what did not;
6. what Square Social knows vs does not know;
7. KEEP / STOP / TEST / SCALE decisions;
8. three plain-English recommendations for the next month.

Use cautious attribution language. Prefer “associated with” or “during this period” unless the data supports a stronger causal claim.

## 9. Revision and communication boundaries

Default service design:
- asynchronous intake and approvals;
- scheduled strategy/check-in touchpoints only when needed;
- one organized monthly approval batch;
- a defined number/window for revisions;
- business-hours responses rather than instant-response expectations;
- emergencies are not created by normal social-media posting.

Anything requiring daily real-time monitoring, high-volume customer service, crisis response or continuous community management must be separately scoped and priced—or declined.

## 10. Production delegation rule

When revenue and evidence justify production help, delegate repeatable execution first:
- design assembly;
- video editing;
- formatting;
- scheduling preparation;
- transcript/caption cleanup;
- asset organization.

Keep founder ownership of:
- strategy;
- client positioning;
- final factual/brand judgment;
- premium client relationship;
- final quality control.

Do not hire merely because revenue increased. Delegate when actual tracked workload proves the bottleneck.

## 11. Optional paid diagnostic

A fixed-scope diagnostic/audit may be used later as a paid entry point for businesses that are not ready for a retainer.

Rules:
- it must be valuable as a standalone product even if the buyer never becomes a client;
- scope and turnaround are fixed;
- findings are sourced;
- include a prioritized action plan rather than a disguised sales pitch;
- only use client results in future marketing with explicit permission;
- do not let diagnostic volume consume the hours reserved for scalable digital products.

Keep this offer internal/unpublished until demand and delivery capacity justify launching it.

## 12. Scaling rule

Square Social does **not** scale premium services by adding unlimited clients.

Growth order:
1. improve three-client pricing and scope;
2. improve digital-product conversion;
3. release self-paced education;
4. build recurring Vault revenue;
5. automate onboarding, delivery and reporting;
6. delegate repeatable production when evidence supports it.

The three-client ceiling remains the default business design unless the owner explicitly changes the model after reviewing actual capacity data.
