## 2. Content strategy: reuse before create
Start every content cycle with an inventory of what already exists.

Sources:
- customer questions,
- product/course material,
- workbooks,
- old posts,
- emails,
- website copy,
- support questions,
- sales objections,
- screen demonstrations,
- existing videos,
- real reviews or UGC with permission.

### Keep / Update / Repurpose / Retire
Every existing asset gets one status:
- **Keep** — still accurate and useful.
- **Update** — useful but needs a current example, CTA or format.
- **Repurpose** — strong idea that can work in another format/channel.
- **Retire** — outdated, off-positioning, duplicative or unsupported.

## 3. One-core-asset cascade
Default campaign production should begin with one substantial core idea.

Example cascade:
1. One original resource article or tutorial
2. One 5–10 minute teaching video or screen demonstration
3. Three short-form videos from the strongest sub-points
4. One carousel/checklist
5. Three to five Pinterest graphics when the topic is evergreen/searchable
6. One useful email
7. One free-resource or product CTA
8. One follow-up FAQ/objection post based on responses

Not every idea needs every format. The purpose is to avoid creating eight unrelated ideas when one good idea can do eight jobs.

## 5. Copy structures
Use the smallest structure that fits the asset.

### Social promotional copy
**Support → Provide → Direct**
- Support the visual with a hook.
- Provide useful context/value.
- Direct to one next step.

### Educational caption
**Hook → Value → Example → Action**

### Short video
**Hook → 3 Points → Next Step**

### Story-based teaching
**Situation → Tension/problem → Useful shift → Example → Next step**

Avoid turning every story into a dramatic personal narrative. The customer’s problem and progress can be the story.

### Accessibility + usability
- Add accurate captions/subtitles.
- Important meaning must not depend only on audio.
- Use readable on-screen text and mobile-safe margins.
- Keep contrast high.
- Avoid overly fast text transitions.
- Use a transcript for longer educational video when practical.
- Keep AI voice/avatar as production support, not the value proposition.

### Platform adaptation
Do not simply crop one master video without checking legibility. Reframe the visual, captions and CTA for the actual destination.

## 7. SEO/resource content checklist
Before publishing an evergreen resource:
- answer one clear search/customer question,
- choose one primary topic/keyword theme,
- use a descriptive title,
- write a useful meta description,
- use one H1 and logical H2/H3 structure,
- link to related Square Social resources/offers,
- use descriptive link text,
- add alt text for meaningful images,
- compress images,
- make the page mobile-readable,
- include one natural CTA,
- update outdated examples rather than publishing duplicate pages.

SEO content exists to answer useful questions and attract qualified demand—not to stuff keywords.

## 8. Campaign landing-page standard
Use a dedicated landing page when traffic is coming from a focused campaign, ad, lead magnet or launch.

Required:
- benefit-led headline,
- offer explained immediately,
- one primary CTA above the fold,
- relevant visual/preview,
- only necessary form fields,
- minimal or no global navigation when distraction would reduce conversion,
- mobile-first layout,
- search metadata where the page should be indexed,
- trust/FAQ details as needed,
- clear privacy/consent language,
- dedicated thank-you/next-step page,
- conversion measurement plan.

Do not remove navigation from normal informational pages; this rule applies to focused conversion pages.

## 9. Campaign workflow

### Before
1. Complete campaign brief.
2. Confirm offer and delivery flow.
3. Audit existing assets.
4. Choose the core asset.
5. Create landing/destination page.
6. Build channel adaptations.
7. QA mobile, links, captions, accessibility and checkout.
8. Define measurement and stop conditions.

### During
1. Monitor delivery/checkout errors first.
2. Monitor questions and objections.
3. Capture recurring comments for future content.
4. Respond to genuine customer questions.
5. Avoid changing multiple major variables at once.

### After
1. Compare result to the defined KPI.
2. Record what worked and what did not.
3. Identify the strongest asset/message.
4. Decide what to keep, update, repurpose or retire.
5. Add new customer questions to the content inventory.
6. Run one focused experiment next time.

## 10. Social listening loop
Once per month, review:
- comments and DMs,
- support questions,
- email replies,
- product/course questions,
- search terms where available,
- public reviews,
- competitor/category questions and gaps,
- frequently repeated language from target customers.

Use listening to identify language and problems—not to copy competitors.

Output:
- 5 recurring questions,
- 3 objections,
- 3 content gaps,
- 1 product improvement opportunity,
- 1 campaign theme for the next cycle.

## 11. Sustainable production cadence
The business should favor batching.

Suggested weekly allocation:
- one strategy/measurement block,
- one core-content production block,
- one adaptation/scheduling block,
- one product/course improvement block,
- one customer/admin block.

If content production consistently exceeds the owner-hours budget, reduce channel count or output volume before adding more work.
