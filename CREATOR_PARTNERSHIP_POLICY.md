# Square Social Studio — Creator & Influencer Partnership Policy

Creator or influencer marketing is optional for Square Social Studio. It should only be used when it can reach a clearly defined small-business audience more efficiently than the existing owned/earned channels.

## 1. Partnership gate
Do not start a creator partnership unless:
- the promoted offer is complete and delivers correctly,
- the offer/message has some evidence of demand or conversion,
- the target customer is clearly defined,
- the creator’s audience overlaps meaningfully with that target,
- the campaign has one primary objective and CTA,
- the destination page and measurement plan are ready,
- the budget and owner-hours requirement are acceptable.

Follower count alone is not a reason to partner.

## 2. Creator selection
Evaluate:
- audience fit,
- content quality,
- brand/personality fit,
- credibility with small-business owners,
- typical engagement quality rather than raw volume,
- professionalism and communication,
- reputation and past partnerships,
- whether their content style can explain Square Social accurately,
- whether their audience can realistically afford/use the promoted offer,
- evidence of authentic rather than manufactured engagement.

Prefer a smaller creator with strong target-market fit over a larger creator with an irrelevant audience.

## 3. Campaign role
Every creator activation needs one defined funnel role:
- Discover / awareness,
- Learn / education,
- Trust / consideration,
- Act / conversion,
- Retain / customer education,
- Advocate / authentic customer storytelling.

Choose metrics that match the role. Do not judge an awareness collaboration only by direct sales, or a conversion collaboration only by views.

## 4. Written agreement required before content creation
The agreement should define at minimum:

### Parties
Clearly identify Square Social Studio and the creator/contracting entity.

### Scope of work
Specify:
- number of posts/videos/stories/emails or other assets,
- platforms,
- format/aspect ratio where relevant,
- required messaging points,
- prohibited/unsubstantiated claims,
- CTA and destination.

### Timeline
Define:
- draft date,
- review date,
- publication dates/window,
- campaign end date,
- reporting deadline.

### Compensation
Define:
- fee/product/commission arrangement,
- payment timing,
- expenses if any,
- tax/invoicing expectations.

### Content and usage rights
Define:
- who owns the original content,
- whether Square Social may repost it,
- whether it may be used on the website, email, landing pages or paid ads,
- how long usage rights last,
- territory/platform restrictions,
- whether editing/cropping/subtitling is permitted,
- whether the creator’s name/image can be used outside the original post.

Do not assume a creator post automatically gives the business unlimited advertising rights.

### Approval process
Define:
- what requires approval,
- number/timing of review rounds,
- who gives final approval,
- what cannot be changed after approval without mutual agreement.

Approvals should ensure accuracy and legal/brand compliance without forcing a creator to give a fake personal opinion.

### Disclosure and legal/platform compliance
Require clear disclosure of material relationships/compensation in the manner required by applicable law, advertising standards and platform rules.

Creator content must also follow Square Social standards for:
- truthful claims,
- privacy/permissions,
- copyright/licensing,
- accessibility where applicable,
- no invented testimonials/results,
- no deceptive urgency.

### Termination
Define when either party may end the agreement, including material breach, non-delivery, serious reputation/safety concerns or inability to publish legally/compliantly.

## 5. Creative brief
The creator receives a short brief containing:
- target audience,
- customer problem,
- offer/product facts,
- approved price/inclusions,
- brand positioning,
- one CTA,
- claims that must not be made,
- disclosure expectation,
- destination link/tracking method,
- campaign KPI.

Do not script a fake testimonial. If the creator is sharing personal experience, it must be their genuine experience.

## 6. Measurement
Possible metrics by objective:

### Awareness
- qualified reach,
- new target-audience visits,
- branded/search interest,
- resource visits.

### Consideration/trust
- engaged visits,
- resource downloads,
- email subscriptions,
- product/course page exploration,
- useful questions/replies.

### Conversion
- tracked checkout clicks,
- purchases,
- conversion rate,
- revenue,
- customer acquisition cost/return on spend when calculable.

### Advocacy
- earned shares,
- permissioned UGC,
- referrals,
- authentic reviews/recommendations.

Record the result in `MEASUREMENT_EXPERIMENT_PLAN.md` and decide whether to repeat, change or stop.

## 7. Affiliate/commission relationships
If Square Social later uses affiliate creators:
- commission terms must be documented,
- tracking must be transparent and tested,
- material relationship disclosure is required,
- refund/chargeback treatment must be defined,
- affiliates cannot make claims Square Social itself could not substantiate.

## 8. Customer creators and UGC
A customer voluntarily posting about a Square Social product is different from a paid creator campaign.

Before Square Social republishes customer content:
- obtain permission,
- preserve context,
- avoid exposing private information,
- disclose incentives if one existed,
- do not convert an ordinary customer comment into a broader endorsement without permission.

See `CUSTOMER_RETENTION_REPUTATION_PLAYBOOK.md` for the UGC process.

## 9. Capacity rule
Creator partnerships should create leverage, not another high-maintenance service line.

If the relationship requires constant calls, manual coordination or custom deliverables that exceed the likely return, do not proceed or redesign the partnership.
