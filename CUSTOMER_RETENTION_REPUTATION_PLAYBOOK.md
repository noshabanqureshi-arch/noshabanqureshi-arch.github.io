# Square Social Studio — Customer Retention, Community & Reputation Playbook

This file applies the course strategies for customer relationships, loyalty, advocacy, community, UGC, reviews, customer service and reputation management.

## 1. Principle
A purchase is not the end of the funnel.

The post-purchase goal is:
**Delivery → Use → Outcome → Support → Feedback → Retention → Advocacy**

Advocacy must be earned. It cannot be manufactured with fake testimonials, paid positive reviews or pressure.

## 2. Post-purchase customer journey

### Immediately
- Deliver the product/access clearly.
- Explain exactly what happens next.
- Make support/contact information easy to find.
- Do not overwhelm the buyer with unrelated upsells before they can access what they purchased.

### Early-use follow-up
For products/courses, send useful implementation guidance appropriate to the offer:
- where to start,
- one quick win,
- common mistake to avoid,
- where to get help.

### Outcome check
After enough time to reasonably use the product:
- “Did this help you solve the problem you bought it for?”
- “What was the most useful part?”
- “What was confusing or missing?”

Use the answers to improve the product and future content.

### Next offer
Only recommend a next product when it solves the next logical problem.

Example:
Free ideas → $27 monthly planning → $69 guided implementation → $149 toolkit → $349 flagship → Vault / premium service.

## 3. Feedback + NPS-style measurement
Use a lightweight recommendation survey periodically, especially after a course or meaningful service milestone.

Questions:
1. On a 0–10 scale, how likely are you to recommend Square Social Studio to another small-business owner?
2. What is the main reason for your score?

Optional third question:
3. What is one thing we could make easier or more useful?

Use the comments more than the vanity of the score itself.

Do not publicly claim an NPS without enough responses and a documented calculation.

## 4. Review and testimonial rules

### Ask honestly
A review request may ask for an **honest** review. Never ask only happy customers to pretend an experience was better than it was.

### Permission
Before using a customer quote, review screenshot, name, photo, business name, logo, result or story in Square Social marketing:
- confirm permission,
- record where the customer approved it for use,
- preserve the meaning of the original statement,
- disclose material incentives or sponsorships when applicable.

### No invented proof
Never invent:
- customers,
- testimonials,
- revenue results,
- follower growth,
- case studies,
- credentials,
- client logos.

Hypothetical examples must be clearly labelled as examples.

## 5. User-generated content (UGC)

### Valuable UGC may include
- an honest review,
- a customer showing how they used a planner/template,
- a before/after workflow example,
- a social post mentioning a resource,
- a voluntary course completion/result story,
- a community discussion or shared tip.

### UGC usage checklist
Before resharing:
1. Is the content authentic and relevant?
2. Does Square Social have permission to reuse it?
3. Are any claims accurate and appropriately contextualized?
4. Is there private or sensitive information visible?
5. Does the content align with brand and safety standards?
6. Is any compensation or incentive disclosed where required?

### Moderation
Do not automatically publish customer submissions to public pages. Review first.

Remove or decline content that contains:
- private personal information,
- harassment or hate,
- unsafe instructions,
- copyright/IP concerns,
- fraudulent claims,
- spam,
- content unrelated to the community purpose.

## 6. Vault community design
If the Square Social Vault includes a community layer, its purpose is implementation and mutual support—not endless engagement.

### Community promise
A calm place for small-business owners to turn real business knowledge into useful content and keep the system moving.

### Recurring community rhythm
Keep it lightweight:
- Monthly: “What are customers asking you this month?” prompt
- Monthly: campaign/content planning thread
- Monthly: template/content drop
- Monthly: wins + lessons thread
- Optional: office-hours/Q&A only if it fits capacity

### Community success indicators
- members using assets,
- member retention,
- questions answered,
- useful peer contributions,
- product feedback,
- voluntary referrals/advocacy.

Do not optimize the community for message volume alone.

## 7. Customer-service standards

### Tone
Warm, factual, calm, solution-focused.

### Response process
1. Identify the actual problem.
2. Confirm what can be fixed.
3. Give one clear next step.
4. Do not argue publicly.
5. Move account/order-specific details to a private channel.
6. Document recurring issues and fix the root cause.

### Escalate immediately when
- a payment/security issue is alleged,
- customer information may have been exposed,
- a public accusation could create legal/reputation risk,
- a safety issue is alleged,
- impersonation/fraud is occurring,
- a widespread delivery or platform outage affects many customers.

## 8. Reputation monitoring
At least monthly, review:
- public brand mentions,
- reviews,
- comments on brand social accounts,
- support themes,
- product refund/delivery complaints,
- unexpected use of brand content or products,
- impersonation/scam reports.

Track:
- issue,
- date,
- channel,
- severity,
- response,
- outcome,
- prevention change.

## 9. Crisis levels

### Level 1 — normal service issue
Examples: download question, typo, small product confusion.

Action: fix quickly, respond directly, record pattern if repeated.

### Level 2 — public complaint or misinformation
Examples: public dissatisfaction, incorrect product claim circulating, repeated negative experience.

Action:
- verify facts,
- acknowledge without defensiveness,
- correct what is known,
- move personal/order details private,
- record the issue and prevention step.

### Level 3 — material reputation/privacy/security/legal risk
Examples: alleged privacy breach, fraudulent account, serious legal allegation, widespread platform/payment failure.

Action:
- stop scheduled promotional posts if they would be inappropriate,
- preserve records,
- centralize response ownership,
- do not speculate,
- communicate verified facts only,
- obtain appropriate professional/platform support when needed,
- publish updates only when useful and accurate.

## 10. Crisis response structure
When a public response is necessary:
1. **Acknowledge** — recognize the concern without arguing.
2. **Facts** — state only what is verified.
3. **Action** — explain what is being done now.
4. **Next update** — say where customers can find further information if more is expected.

Never use a crisis to create promotional content.

## 11. Social media policy for anyone representing the brand
- Protect customer information.
- Do not disclose private client work without permission.
- Do not make unverified claims.
- Do not post from anger or during an unresolved dispute.
- Separate personal opinions from official brand statements.
- Respect copyright and licensed assets.
- Disclose sponsorship/material relationships where applicable.
- Correct meaningful errors transparently.
- Use approved brand voice and visual system.
- Escalate potential legal, privacy or security issues rather than improvising publicly.

## 12. Advocacy loop
The ideal loop is:

**Useful product → successful use → helpful support → honest feedback → product improvement → voluntary review/referral → new qualified customer.**

This is more sustainable than constantly increasing ad spend or client volume.
