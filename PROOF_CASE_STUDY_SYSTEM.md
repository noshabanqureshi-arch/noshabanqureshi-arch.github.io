# Square Social Studio — Proof & Case Study System

Purpose: build credible evidence over time without inventing testimonials, overstating attribution or publishing customer information without permission.

## Rule 1 — No empty social-proof theatre
Do not add:
- fake testimonials,
- placeholder logos that imply clients,
- invented star ratings,
- “trusted by hundreds” claims without evidence,
- screenshots from test/simulated clients presented as real,
- revenue/follower claims that cannot be traced to source data.

A clean site with no testimonials is more credible than fabricated proof.

## Rule 2 — Separate four evidence types

### A. Product usability evidence
Examples:
- customer successfully accessed/downloaded the product,
- customer completed the planning task,
- customer reports a specific section was useful,
- support/access issue rate.

This does not prove business results.

### B. Customer outcome evidence
Examples:
- customer reports reduced planning time,
- customer completed a month of content,
- customer published more consistently,
- customer understood their content pillars better.

State the outcome narrowly and accurately.

### C. Marketing performance evidence
Examples:
- qualified traffic,
- leads/inquiries,
- bookings,
- sales,
- campaign conversion.

Only use actual platform/business data the customer provided or authorized.
Use cautious attribution language unless causality can genuinely be supported.

### D. Service-quality evidence
Examples:
- delivered on time,
- approval process was clear,
- communication was efficient,
- reporting was understandable,
- client renewed/refers.

This is often useful proof even before large marketing-result samples exist.

# Product feedback flow
After a customer has had enough time to use a product, ask:
1. What problem were you trying to solve when you bought it?
2. Did you finish the intended task?
3. What part was most useful?
4. What felt confusing or unnecessary?
5. What changed in how you plan/create content?
6. What would you improve?

Then, separately:
7. Would you be comfortable leaving an honest public review?
8. May Square Social quote any part of your feedback publicly?
9. If yes, how may you be identified: first name only, first name + business, business only, or anonymous?

Do not merge feedback permission and public-use permission into one vague checkbox.

# Done-for-You evidence timeline

## Month 1
Focus:
- onboarding quality,
- baseline data,
- delivery process,
- content system established.

Do not rush a testimonial request.

## Month 2
Focus:
- delivery consistency,
- early performance patterns,
- client friction/support burden,
- evidence quality.

## Month 3+
If the client has enough experience:
- ask for structured feedback,
- ask whether an honest review is appropriate,
- request case-study permission separately,
- collect exact source data for any published metrics.

# Case study template

## 1. Context
- business type,
- audience,
- relevant starting situation,
- timeframe.

Only include details the customer has approved for publication.

## 2. Problem
What operational/marketing problem was the business trying to solve?

## 3. Starting evidence
Where available:
- current workflow,
- posting consistency,
- content inventory,
- qualified traffic/inquiries/bookings,
- other relevant baseline.

If baseline data was unavailable, say so.

## 4. Approach
Describe what Square Social actually did:
- source-material audit,
- positioning/content pillars,
- customer journey,
- content production,
- landing page/resource,
- publishing workflow,
- measurement.

## 5. Output
What was delivered?

## 6. Results
Use actual data only.
Split:
- business outcomes,
- marketing outcomes,
- workflow/operational outcomes.

Use language such as “during the period,” “associated with,” or “the client reported” when appropriate rather than implying causation that cannot be proven.

## 7. What we learned
Include what did not work or what changed. A useful case study should not read like an advertisement pretending every tactic won.

## 8. Customer quote
Only an exact or lightly edited quote with explicit publication permission.

## 9. Evidence record
Internally store:
- source screenshots/exports,
- dates,
- calculation method,
- customer approval,
- permitted identification/use.

Do not store private evidence in public GitHub.

# Public proof hierarchy
As evidence becomes available, prefer this order:
1. clear product/service scope,
2. transparent process,
3. genuine customer feedback,
4. verified workflow outcomes,
5. verified business/marketing outcomes,
6. case studies.

Do not jump directly to dramatic result claims.

# Review publication rules
- Do not require a positive rating.
- Do not offer an undisclosed incentive for praise.
- If an incentive is offered for feedback, it must not require positivity and any public disclosure requirements must be respected.
- Do not edit a quote in a way that changes meaning.
- Get permission for customer name/business/image/logo separately.
- Remove proof if permission is withdrawn where required/appropriate.

# Proof section launch gate
Do not create a public “Results” or “Testimonials” section until there are at least a few genuine proof assets that improve the visitor's decision.

Until then, trust should come from:
- useful free teaching,
- transparent pricing/status,
- clear boundaries,
- secure provider checkout,
- customer-success guidance,
- accurate claims,
- professional process.
