# Square Social Studio — Quarterly Strategy Review

Use this review every three months, or earlier if there is a major change in the market, business model, platform environment or owner capacity.

The purpose is to keep Square Social Studio aligned with real evidence rather than adding products, channels or work reactively.

## 2. SWOT analysis
Keep this focused on the big picture.

### Strengths — internal
Ask:
- What does Square Social do especially well?
- What clearly distinguishes the brand from competitors?
- Which assets, systems or intellectual property are difficult to copy well?
- Which offers produce strong value without excessive owner time?
- Which content or customer experiences are earning trust?

Record the top 3–5 strengths.

### Weaknesses — internal
Ask:
- Where is the business still dependent on manual owner work?
- Which offers create disproportionate support or production time?
- Where is the customer journey confusing or incomplete?
- Which skills, systems, proof or assets are still missing?
- Which channel is consuming effort without business impact?

Record the top 3–5 weaknesses and one action for each material weakness.

### Opportunities — external
Ask:
- What small-business marketing problems are still poorly served?
- Which recurring customer questions could become products, resources or courses?
- Are there underserved business niches that fit the Square Social method?
- What search/content topics show qualified demand?
- Are there partnerships, affiliates, referrals or creator collaborations that could reach the right audience efficiently?
- Is there a product format customers are asking for repeatedly?

Record the top 3–5 opportunities and rank them by fit, potential, reachability and owner-hours required.

### Threats — external
Ask:
- What competitors or substitute tools are improving quickly?
- Are platform or algorithm changes weakening a channel?
- Are AI/content tools commoditizing a low-value part of the offer?
- Are laws, privacy requirements, advertising rules or platform terms changing?
- Is economic pressure changing small-business willingness to spend?
- Is a third-party platform creating delivery/payment risk?

Record the top 3–5 threats and the mitigation for each material threat.

## 3. Competitive/category review
This is not a copying exercise.

Review a small sample of relevant alternatives and note:
- target customer,
- core promise,
- product/service ladder,
- pricing visibility,
- strongest content themes,
- customer proof,
- delivery model,
- apparent strengths,
- obvious gaps,
- what Square Social should deliberately **not** imitate.

Then answer:
1. Is Square Social still clearly positioned?
2. Is “turn what you already know into content” still distinctive and useful?
3. Are we competing on a meaningful customer benefit rather than features alone?
4. Has the market created a better opportunity to narrow the target segment?

## 4. STP health check
### Segment
Which customer segment produced the strongest combination of:
- demand,
- conversion,
- satisfaction,
- profitability,
- low support burden?

### Target
Should Square Social prioritize:
- DIY Starter,
- Guided Builder,
- Premium Outsourcer,
- or a narrower subsegment within one of these?

### Position
Can a target customer understand in one sentence:
- who this is for,
- what problem it solves,
- why Square Social is different?

If not, revise positioning before expanding the catalogue.

## 5. 7P business health check
Score each area 1–5 and record one improvement if below 4.

### Product
Are the offers useful, differentiated and connected logically?

### Price
Does the price reflect perceived value, customer ability to pay and business economics?

### Place
Can customers discover, purchase and access offers easily?

### Promotion
Are campaigns attracting the right people and producing measurable movement through the funnel?

### People
Is anyone representing the brand trained on voice, support, privacy and quality standards?

### Process
Are checkout, delivery, onboarding, approvals and support low-friction and documented?

### Physical Evidence
Does the website, product quality, customer experience and real proof make the business credible?

## 6. Funnel health
Review conversion or meaningful movement at each stage:
- Discover
- Learn
- Trust
- Act
- Retain
- Advocate

Identify the single weakest handoff.

Do not redesign the whole funnel at once. Choose one bottleneck to improve next quarter.

## 7. Customer voice review
Summarize the quarter’s:
- five most common questions,
- three most common objections,
- three support problems,
- strongest positive feedback themes,
- product/course improvement requests,
- referral/review themes,
- one unmet need worth testing.

Use actual customer language where permission/privacy allows internal use.

## 8. Content/channel review
For each active channel record:
- role in the funnel,
- qualified traffic/leads/revenue assisted,
- content effort,
- owner hours,
- strongest format,
- whether to Keep / Improve / Reduce / Stop.

Do not keep a channel solely because follower count is growing.

## 9. Capacity review
The owner operating guardrail remains a strategic constraint.

Ask:
- Are average working hours staying within the planned ceiling?
- Which recurring tasks should be automated?
- Which work can be templated?
- Which production task can eventually be delegated without losing strategy/quality?
- Is a new offer increasing revenue without proportionally increasing owner hours?
- Are premium client spots still capped appropriately?

If revenue growth requires continuously increasing owner hours, redesign the offer before scaling it.

## 10. Experiment review
List the quarter’s A/B tests and what was learned.

For each:
- hypothesis,
- variable tested,
- metric,
- result,
- decision,
- follow-up test.

Promote proven lessons into the permanent playbooks.

## 11. Quarterly decisions
End every review with no more than five decisions:

1. **Double down:** one thing already working.
2. **Fix:** the largest funnel/customer-experience bottleneck.
3. **Test:** one opportunity.
4. **Stop/reduce:** one low-value activity.
5. **Protect:** one strength, asset or trust factor that must not be diluted.

## 12. New initiative gate
Before approving a new product, platform, creator partnership, ad campaign or service:
- Is there evidence of customer need?
- Does it fit the target segment and positioning?
- Does it connect to the existing offer ladder?
- Can the business measure success?
- Does it fit the owner-hours constraint?
- Can it use/reuse existing intellectual property?
- What will be stopped or deprioritized to make room for it?

If the answers are weak, park the idea rather than adding it to the workload.
