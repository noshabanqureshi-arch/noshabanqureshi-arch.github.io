Content strategy · 7 minute read

Give every post one job.

Small-business content gets easier to plan when every post is not expected to educate, entertain, prove credibility and sell at the same time. Use five simple jobs: Teach, Trust, Connect, Offer and Convert.

1. Teach

Teach

Answer, explain or simplify.

Teach content helps a customer understand a problem, decision or process. Use FAQs, mistakes, myths, checklists, comparisons and beginner explanations.

Example: A bookkeeper explains three records a new business owner should keep organized before tax season.

2. Trust

Trust

Show how the business thinks and works.

Trust content makes standards, process and judgment visible. Show how you make decisions, prepare work, check quality or protect the customer experience.

Example: A cleaning company explains the checklist used before a team leaves a home.

3. Connect

Connect

Make the business human without oversharing.

Connect content can share values, lessons, useful stories and relevant opinions. The goal is not random personal content; it is helping people understand the perspective behind the business.

Example: A consultant explains why she stopped recommending a complicated process that looked impressive but created more work for clients.

4. Offer

Offer

Explain what you sell.

Offer content helps people understand who an offer is for, what is included, what happens next, common objections and where it fits compared with other options.

Example: A photographer explains what is included in a mini-session and who should choose a full session instead.

5. Convert

Convert

Make one next step clear.

Convert content gives a direct action: download, buy, book, inquire, join or register. Conversion content works better when earlier content has already helped the audience understand the problem and offer.

Example: A local studio invites people to register for a real upcoming workshop with a clear date and registration link.

A balanced month does not mean equal numbers.

Your business may need more teaching this month and more offer content next month. Use the mix that supports the current goal instead of forcing an arbitrary ratio.

Use one primary job and one primary CTA.

A post can have secondary effects, but planning becomes clearer when you decide the main job before writing. If the job is Teach, the CTA might be “save this checklist.” If the job is Convert, the CTA might be “book the consultation.”

Planning shortcut: Before creating a post, write three things at the top of the draft: audience, content job and next step.
