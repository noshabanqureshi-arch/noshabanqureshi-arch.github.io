Planning · 7 minute read

Plan the month before you start creating the posts.

Opening Canva or writing captions too early creates unnecessary decisions. A simpler monthly workflow makes the strategic choices first, then uses those choices to speed up production.

Use this order:
Goal → Audience → Pillars → Idea Bank → Calendar → Create.
Step 1

Choose one business goal.

What should the content help the business accomplish this month? Examples: explain a new offer, generate qualified inquiries, drive downloads, educate customers before booking or support a real launch.

Step 2

Choose the audience you are speaking to.

Write the customer’s problem, desired outcome, common objections and questions. Do not try to speak to every possible customer in every post.

Step 3

Choose a few repeatable content pillars.

Useful pillars can include the problem you solve, your process, customer questions/objections and the offer/use cases. Repeat the pillars; change the angle.

Step 4

Build an idea bank inside the pillars.

Generate more ideas than you need. Pull from real questions, notes, old posts, sales conversations, documents and existing explanations before brainstorming from zero.

Step 5

Put only realistic ideas on the calendar.

Thirty ideas do not require thirty posts. Choose the rhythm the business can maintain and save unused ideas for later.

Step 6

Create in batches.

Group similar work: write several captions together, design related graphics together and film multiple short videos in one session. Batch decisions before batch production.

Step 7

Review the evidence.

At month end, look at business-linked outcomes first, then platform diagnostics. Decide what to keep, stop, test or scale instead of automatically building a completely new strategy.

What this workflow prevents

It reduces random posting, overproduction, platform hopping, inconsistent calls to action and the feeling that each Monday requires a new social-media strategy.

What to prepare before the planning session

Have the current offer, customer questions, recent performance signals, existing content worth reusing and any real upcoming dates or promotions in front of you. The better the source material, the less generic the plan becomes.

Rule: The calendar is the output of the strategy—not the strategy itself.
