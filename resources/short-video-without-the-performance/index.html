Short-form video · 7 minute read

You can make useful short video without turning yourself into a performer.

A small business does not need complicated filming, daily trends or high-energy camera presence to use short-form video. It needs a useful idea, a format that fits the message and a production process it can repeat.

Start with the job of the video.

Before choosing a trend, transition or editing style, decide what the video is supposed to do.

  • Discover: help the right person recognize a problem or question.
  • Learn: explain one useful idea clearly.
  • Trust: show your process, standards, reasoning or behind-the-scenes evidence.
  • Act: explain the offer or give one clear next step.

A video that tries to do all four jobs at once usually becomes cluttered.

Five formats that do not require “performing.”

Voiceover + B-roll

Film your workspace, process, product, hands, tools or screen while a short voiceover explains the idea.

Screen recording

Useful for showing a process, template, website, planning system, before/after structure or software workflow.

Text-led video

Use strong on-screen text over simple footage when the idea is short enough to understand without narration.

Talking-head answer

Answer one customer question directly. Keep it conversational rather than trying to sound like an advertisement.

Carousel-to-video

Turn an existing list or carousel into timed slides with captions or voiceover instead of creating a new concept.

FAQ series

Use the same visual structure repeatedly and change only the question. Repeatability is the advantage.

A simple 30-second framework

Hook → Value → Example → Action

  1. Hook: name the useful problem or outcome quickly.
  2. Value: explain the main idea in plain language.
  3. Example: make it concrete with a real situation, process or demonstration.
  4. Action: give one next step if one is needed.

The sections do not have to be equal length. Sometimes the hook is one sentence and the useful explanation is the rest of the video.

Make it work with the sound off.

Short social video is often encountered before someone decides whether to listen. Use readable captions or on-screen text so the core idea is still understandable without sound.

Caption quality is part of production quality. Check spelling, timing, contrast and whether text is hidden by platform interface elements.

Batch the format, not just the filming.

Instead of trying to invent six different video styles, choose one or two repeatable formats and record several ideas inside them.

For example:

  • four FAQ talking-head clips in one session;
  • six voiceovers using footage captured during normal work;
  • three screen-recording tutorials from one longer walkthrough;
  • several text-led clips from one existing article or carousel.

One useful idea can become more than one asset.

A strong customer question can become:

  • one 30-second video;
  • one carousel;
  • one longer resource article;
  • one email;
  • one FAQ on the sales page;
  • a lesson inside a future course.

The goal is not to create more raw ideas. The goal is to get more useful mileage from the ones worth saying.

Measure the video based on its job.

Do not judge every video only by views.

  • Discover video: qualified reach, profile visits, new relevant viewers.
  • Learn video: watch time, completion, saves, useful comments/questions.
  • Trust video: profile/site actions, DMs, repeat viewers and qualitative response.
  • Act video: clicks, inquiries, downloads, checkout visits or purchases where trackable.
