AI + small-business content · 7 minute read

Use AI to organize your expertise—not manufacture it.

AI can make social-media production faster, but the fastest way to sound generic is to ask it to invent the whole message. A better workflow starts with what your business actually knows.

The problem with “write me a viral post.”

That prompt gives the tool almost nothing useful to work with. It does not know your real customer question, your actual answer, the example you would use, the words your customers use, the limits of your offer or what you want the post to accomplish.

The result may be polished, but polished is not the same as credible.

Square Social rule: AI should reduce blank-page friction. It should not replace judgment, facts, examples or the parts of the message customers need from the business itself.

A five-part input that produces better drafts

  1. The real question. Start with something a customer actually asks or needs to understand.
  2. Your actual answer. Explain it in rough notes, voice-to-text or bullet points before asking AI to polish it.
  3. A real example. Add a process, situation, product detail or example you can verify.
  4. The job of the content. Is this piece meant to help someone Discover, Learn, Trust or Act?
  5. The guardrails. Tell the tool what it must not invent or overstate.

A practical prompt

Try this: Here is a customer question: [paste it]. Here is my actual answer in rough notes: [paste your notes]. Here is one real example I can stand behind: [paste example]. Turn this into a clear social-media post for [platform]. The primary job is [Discover / Learn / Trust / Act]. Keep the tone [describe your voice]. Do not invent statistics, testimonials, credentials, customer results or facts I did not provide. If the post would be stronger with a real example or proof I have not supplied, flag that gap for me instead of filling it in.

Then use AI for the repetitive parts.

Once the core idea is accurate, AI becomes much more useful. It can help you:

  • turn the same answer into a Reel script, carousel outline and email;
  • shorten or clarify a draft;
  • generate several hooks for the same verified message;
  • organize customer questions into content pillars;
  • repurpose a long explanation into smaller pieces;
  • check whether the CTA is clear;
  • create a first-pass calendar from ideas you already approved.

What should stay human?

Keep final control over factual accuracy, claims, professional advice, regulated language, customer privacy, examples, brand judgment and anything that could materially affect a buying decision.

If AI adds a number, result, testimonial, credential or “customer story” that you did not provide, remove it or verify it before publishing.

A simple before-you-publish check

  • Could I explain every factual statement if a customer asked?
  • Does this sound like how the business actually thinks?
  • Is there a useful idea here, or just polished filler?
  • Did AI invent any proof?
  • Is there one clear next step?
