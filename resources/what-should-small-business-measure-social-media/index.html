Measurement · 7 minute read

Measure what the content is supposed to help the business do.

Likes, views and follower count can tell you something, but they are not the business outcome by themselves. A useful measurement system starts with the goal, then chooses the smallest set of signals that help you make a decision.

Start with the business outcome.

Before opening an analytics dashboard, ask what the business actually wanted the month of content to support.

Examples might include:

  • more qualified inquiries;
  • more calls or bookings;
  • more downloads of a useful resource;
  • more product-page visits or purchases;
  • more repeat customers;
  • more people understanding a new service or offer.

Once the outcome is clear, social metrics become evidence rather than decoration.

Use two layers of metrics.

1. Outcome signals

Inquiries, calls, bookings, purchases, downloads, email signups, direction requests, quote requests, repeat purchase or another agreed business action.

2. Diagnostic signals

Reach, watch time, completion, saves, shares, profile visits, link clicks, comments and other platform data that help explain why an outcome may or may not be happening.

Outcome signals tell you whether the content appears to be helping the business. Diagnostic signals help you decide what to change.

Match the metric to the content job.

Discover

Relevant reach, non-follower discovery, search visibility, profile visits and qualified new traffic.

Learn

Watch time, completion, saves, shares and questions that show the content was useful enough to keep or discuss.

Trust

Repeat engagement, profile/site actions, meaningful DMs, FAQ behavior and movement toward higher-intent pages.

Act

CTA clicks, downloads, inquiries, bookings, checkout visits and completed purchases where they can be measured.

Do not force attribution the data cannot prove.

If a customer saw three Reels, visited the website twice, received a referral from a friend and then called, it may be impossible to credit the sale to one post with certainty.

Use careful language such as:

  • “associated with an increase in…”
  • “during the same period…”
  • “this post generated the most tracked clicks…”
  • “we do not yet know whether…”

Credible reporting includes what you do not know.

A monthly KEEP / STOP / TEST / SCALE review

Turn numbers into decisions.

  • KEEP: continue something that is working or still useful.
  • STOP: remove something that has repeatedly consumed effort without helping the goal.
  • TEST: change one meaningful variable and define what success would look like before running it.
  • SCALE: give more distribution, budget or production effort to something with enough evidence to justify it.

Test one variable at a time.

If you change the headline, price, image, CTA, audience and landing page at the same time, you may improve the result without learning why.

Useful tests might compare:

  • benefit-led hook vs question-led hook;
  • Reel length;
  • CTA wording;
  • product page vs focused campaign landing page;
  • one visual style vs another;
  • email subject line or send timing.

A small dashboard is better than a giant one nobody uses.

For many small businesses, a monthly snapshot could be enough:

  1. business goal;
  2. 2–3 outcome signals;
  3. 3–5 diagnostic metrics;
  4. top three content pieces by the metric that matched their job;
  5. what we know / what we do not know;
  6. KEEP / STOP / TEST / SCALE;
  7. three next-month decisions.

Vanity metrics are not useless—they are incomplete.

A growing follower count can matter if the followers are relevant. High views can matter if discovery is the goal. Engagement can matter if it represents real interest or useful conversation.

The problem begins when the number becomes the goal without any connection to what the business is trying to accomplish.
